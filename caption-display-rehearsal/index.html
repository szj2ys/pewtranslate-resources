Caption display rehearsal

Try your reviewed cue text at fixed display settings. Step through it yourself, compare how it wraps, and keep an observation log for the next operator.

Nothing is timed or translated. This page does not play media, record sound, or provide a live caption service. It works locally without an account, storage or network connection.

1. Prepare a short cue deck

Use wording already reviewed by the people responsible for it. Keep your original elsewhere. Try the fictional English example to learn the controls.

Both buttons clear all cues, settings, notes and observations. Download your log first if you need it.

Up to 20 cues, 2,000 UTF-16 units per cue, 12,000 across the deck. Removed cues remain in older observation snapshots.

A browser textarea can normalize pasted CRLF/CR line endings to LF. The tool preserves the accepted editor value; it does not change your source file.

2. Choose a display configuration

Colors use six hexadecimal digits, such as #ffffff. This preview does not score contrast or certify accessibility. Its controls keep their own colors.

The box is capped by available page width. Font size never shrinks to fit. CSS pixels are not physical measurements; a narrow preview is not a phone or projector simulation. Use the actual device and viewing position for a real rehearsal.

3. Step, compare and observe

No automatic advance. At the first or last cue, the corresponding button is disabled. Tab to a button and press Enter or Space; there are no global keyboard shortcuts.

    What to check beyond this box

    1. Use the intended device, display, lighting and viewing position. A responsive browser test does not stand in for an actual phone, projector or audience check.
    2. Ask intended readers about their preferences. Font availability, visual needs and a player’s caption styling differ. A box that fits may still be hard to read.
    3. Check real captions against their recording for meaning, speaker identification and relevant sounds. This manual deck cannot check synchronization, reading speed, translation or caption completeness.

    The PewTranslate setup and rehearsal guide explains testing the intended setup with competent human review. W3C’s captions guidance explains the wider captioning task and variation in player styling.
